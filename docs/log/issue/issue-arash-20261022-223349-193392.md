## Problem

A quote that matches only approximately (80% of 3-word shingles) passes lint silently and appears as **verified** in the review packet, like an exact match. Changing one word can invert the claim and still pass. The reviewer has no way to see that the quote was altered, which defeats the point of quotes as the checkable part of a claim.

## Evidence

`biblio.wiki.fidelity.QuoteChecker.check` against `alemanzapata_2026_DeltasSpindles` (source sentence: "C2 ripples were less affected by the cortical plasticity manipulation and became more numerous, ..."):

```
exact | C2 ripples were less affected by the cortical plasticity manipulation ...
fuzzy | C2 ripples were more affected by the cortical plasticity manipulation ...
fuzzy | C2 ripples were not affected by the cortical plasticity manipulation ...
```

On a real page (`docs/wiki/concepts/ripple-subtypes.md:50`) I wrote "identified" where docling has "identif ied": lint gave no finding, and the packet says `line 50, verified: "...longest duration of all identified classes"`.

Expected: exact matches are verified; fuzzy matches are a lint warning, shown in the packet as "approximate" with the differing words; words that change meaning (not, no, less, more, numbers, units) must match exactly.

## Request

- Report `fuzzy` as its own status in lint (warning) and in the packet, with a word diff against the best-matching passage.
- Treat negations, comparatives and number tokens as must-match.
- Normalize the common docling ligature split (`f i`, `f l`: "identif ied", "ref lect") on both sides, so an agent can quote clean text and still get an exact match; then the skill can stop asking for OCR slips to be copied.

## How journal-club will use it

Every claim on a wiki page is backed by a quote, and the reviewer (Arash) relies on the packet's verified mark.
