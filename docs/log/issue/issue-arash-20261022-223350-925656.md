## Problem

Asked to judge whether `biblio wiki review packet` gives a reviewer what they need, on two pages (`concepts/ripple-subtypes.md`, `sources/alemanzapata-2026-deltas-spindles.md`). It is a good start: lede, flags, lint, each quote with a verified mark, where each citation resolves, the accept and flag commands. Missing for a fast and safe judgment:

1. **The claim next to its quote.** The packet lists quotes alone. The reviewer's question is "does this sentence on the page say what the quote says?"; that needs the bullet or sentence the quote backs, side by side with it.
2. **Where in the paper.** The quote's section (and a link to the line in the docling markdown), so a reviewer can read the context in one click.
3. **Exact vs approximate** (R13): today every fuzzy match shows as verified.
4. **Claims without a quote.** Sentences that cite a paper (or state a number) without a blockquote, listed as "unbacked", so the reviewer knows where to look hardest.
5. **Numbers on the page** with where each appears in the source (or "not found"), since numbers are where drafts go wrong.
6. **Embedded figures** (file, annex state, caption).
7. **Outgoing links and the hub(s)** the page belongs to, to judge placement (one idea per page).
8. **"last commit"** shows whichever commit last touched the file; in journal-club that was another session's blanket save with an unrelated message. The page's `author` and the log.md entries say more; show the log entries for the page instead or as well.
9. **Pool-only citations** (CIT003 notes) belong in the packet: they must be added to the project bibliography before rendering.
